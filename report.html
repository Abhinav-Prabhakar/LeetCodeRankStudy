01 The reading

LeetCode’s profile rank is not a contest rating. It is a tally of problems solved — a number that rises when you finish a question, and barely moves when you don’t.

Rank 1 has solved all 4,123 problems. From there the bands compress: the median of the top 500 sits at 3,403 solved, the next 500 at 2,723, the next at 2,421, and the last at 2,216.

“At the top, the problem set runs out before the ambition does.”

By rank 2,000 the curve is almost flat. Between the 1,000th and 2,000th places, a thousand ranks separate users by about 500 problems. At this altitude, rank is less a measure of effort than of time and attrition — and of which problems LeetCode has added this month.

Contest rating moves on a different clock. It is nearly uncorrelated with profile rank here — Pearson r = −0.11, Spearman ρ = −0.13 — and the bands barely separate: the top 500 has a median rating of 2,479, the last 500 of 2,452. The highest rating in the sample, 3,589, belongs to No. 1,577; the second highest, 3,365, to No. 1,964.

Solving every problem and solving problems quickly are different skills. The caveat is range restriction: every profile here is already elite by volume, and all 243 carry a Guardian badge. Across LeetCode as a whole, the relationship is surely stronger.

03 — Method & caveats

How this was made.

Method

LeetCode publishes no leaderboard for the profile rank shown under a username. The only enumerable public list is the contest leaderboard, whose nodes carry each user’s profile rank.

I scanned pages 1–180 of the globalRanking query — 4,500 contestants — kept the 243 with a profile rank of 2,000 or better, then fetched their live solved totals from matchedUser.submitStatsGlobal.

Contest ratings come from the public userContestRanking query for the same 243 usernames. All 243 have a rating: median 2,479, range 2,304–3,589, every one a Guardian. Against profile rank, Pearson r = −0.11, Spearman ρ = −0.13.

Captured 24 November 2026. The full pipeline lives in render.py; the datasets are data.csv and ratings.csv.

Caveats

  • This is a sample, not a census. Users who never entered a contest are invisible to this method, which is why gaps appear at ranks 2, 4, 5 and elsewhere.
  • Profile rank reflects problems solved. The separate “Global Ranking” on a LeetCode profile is contest rating; the two are not the same.
  • The near-zero rating correlation is partly range restriction: every profile here is already in the top 2,000 by solved count, and all 243 carry a Guardian badge. Across LeetCode as a whole, the relationship is surely stronger.
  • At the top, solved counts saturate at 4,123 — the full problem set — so ties are effectively arbitrary.
  • The data moves as LeetCode adds problems. Every figure here is a snapshot.
